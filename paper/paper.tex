\title{Pooled Correlations Hide the Structure:\\
Neural-Collapse Coordinates and Held-Out Accuracy\\
Within Training Conditions}
\author{David Lindgreen}
\date{October 9, 2026 \\[2pt] \small Version 0.1.0 \quad Preprint, not peer reviewed}

\begin{abstract}
Neural collapse describes four geometric regularities (NC1--NC4) of a classifier's last-layer features, often observed late in
training~\cite{papyan2020}. We tested, in a small and fully inspectable setting, whether they continue to strengthen after
interpolation and whether their strength tracks held-out accuracy: a $64\to64\to9\to10$ ReLU network on the official writer-disjoint
UCI Optical Digits split~\cite{uci1998}, ten seeds in each of three training conditions (clean, long-tailed, 20\% label noise).
Two of four preregistered directional gates passed; the re-run of the whole study reproduces the frozen results (largest numeric
difference $3.4\times10^{-6}$, identical gates). The original report quoted a descriptive Spearman association of $-0.518$
between final NC1 and test accuracy over all 30 runs. We show that this pooled figure is produced by the three conditions, not by
NC1: inside each condition the association is $+0.04$, $-0.04$ and $-0.09$, and the pooled within-condition association is
$-0.03$ (95\% seed-bootstrap interval $-0.49$ to $+0.42$). The same holds for NC3 (pooled $-0.75$, within $+0.05$). Only NC4, the
disagreement between the network and nearest-class-centre decisions, is associated with accuracy within conditions
($-0.49$; $-0.80$ to $-0.04$), consistently across all three. This is an instance of Simpson's paradox~\cite{simpson1951}
in a two-variable summary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question}
After a small network reaches zero training error, does its hidden representation continue toward neural-collapse geometry, and
does stronger collapse consistently accompany better generalization to unseen writers? The setting is deliberately small:
3{,}823 training and 1{,}797 test examples from disjoint writers, a multinomial logistic reference, and ten algorithmic seeds
per condition (seeds are replications of the optimizer, not samples of people).

\section{Frozen design and results}
The three conditions were the clean training set, a deterministic long tail (class $c$ keeps $\lfloor n_c10^{-c/9}\rfloor$ rows, at
least 25; 1{,}559 rows) and exactly 20\% symmetric label noise per class (761 changed labels). Models were trained for 600 Adam epochs.
The collapse coordinates are NC1 (within-class variability relative to between-class, lower is more collapsed), NC2 (deviation
of centred class means from the simplex equiangular frame), NC3 (classifier--class-mean misalignment) and NC4 (disagreement with
nearest-class-centre decisions), computed in the full nine-dimensional hidden space.

Clean-condition MLP test accuracy had a median of 96.27\% (95.88--96.94\%), long tail 92.29\%, label noise 81.64\%; the logistic
reference reached 94.77\%, 91.88\% and 93.60\%. The frozen report quoted descriptive pooled Spearman associations of $-0.518$ (NC1)
and $-0.026$ (NC2) with test accuracy over the 30 MLP runs and reported no $p$-values, since seeds are not independent samples of a
population. We re-ran the entire study from the pinned dataset: every number reproduces (largest absolute difference
$3.4\times10^{-6}$ in a class-angle Gram entry), as do all gate counts and both pooled correlations.

\section{Post-hoc: associations within conditions}
The three conditions differ by more than ten accuracy points, so any metric that also differs between conditions will correlate with
accuracy in a pooled sample whether or not it tracks accuracy among comparable runs. We therefore recomputed each coordinate's Spearman
association with final test accuracy (i) pooled, (ii) inside each condition and (iii) pooled within condition (ranks computed inside
each condition), with percentile intervals from a 5{,}000-resample bootstrap over seeds within condition. This analysis was not
preregistered and all of it is reported.
\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
 & Pooled & Within (pooled) & 95\% interval & Clean & Long tail & Label noise\\
\midrule
NC1 & $-0.518$ & $-0.031$ & $-0.49$ to $0.42$ & $+0.04$ & $-0.04$ & $-0.09$\\
NC2 & $-0.026$ & $-0.310$ & $-0.63$ to $0.12$ & $-0.56$ & $-0.24$ & $-0.13$\\
NC3 & $-0.750$ & $+0.052$ & $-0.37$ to $0.46$ & $-0.16$ & $+0.21$ & $+0.13$\\
NC4 & $-0.622$ & $-0.486$ & $-0.80$ to $-0.04$ & $-0.56$ & $-0.44$ & $-0.45$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Spearman association between each final collapse coordinate and test accuracy; each condition has 10 seeds.}
\end{table}
For NC1 and NC3 the pooled association disappears within conditions: the apparent link between ``more collapsed'' and
``more accurate'' is the difference between conditions. NC2 shows a weak, imprecise negative within-condition association
(largest in the clean condition), even though its pooled value is near zero because the conditions pull in opposite directions.
NC4 stands out: its within-condition association is moderately negative in every condition and its pooled-within interval excludes
zero, though each single-condition interval does not.

\section{Discussion}
The frozen conclusion, that a single collapse coordinate is not a general certificate of generalization, is supported and, if
anything, sharpened: NC1 and NC3 are condition markers rather than predictors of accuracy among comparable runs, and the one
coordinate that tracks accuracy within conditions is the decision-level one (NC4). The pooled NC1 figure in the original report should
be read as a between-condition association. The within-condition estimates are imprecise (ten seeds per condition, intervals wide) and
describe seed-to-seed variation in one small architecture, dataset and optimizer; they neither establish nor rule out a causal role
for collapse.

\section{Limitations}
One architecture, one dataset, three hand-built conditions, ten seeds each; collapse coordinates measured at the final epoch only;
the bootstrap resamples seeds, which are not draws from a population of tasks; and the within-condition analysis is exploratory.
